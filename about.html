About

Who I am:

I am a learning designer and psychology researcher. I am currently pursuing a Master of Education in Learning Design, Innovation, and Technology at the Harvard Graduate School of Education, after earning a B.A. in Psychology with a minor in Economics from Emory University. My work sits at the intersection of how people learn, how they feel, and how AI can support both.

Across research labs, an AI startup, and a health-technology company, the throughline has stayed the same: start with a real understanding of the people involved, ground the design in theory and evidence, and measure whether it worked.

My process:

I begin with the learner or user, not the content. In the Hamann Cognitive Science Lab at Emory I spent two years collecting and analyzing data from over 100 participants, which taught me to respect what evidence says over what feels intuitive. That habit carries into product work: at Guanxin I collected and synthesized feedback from 60+ participants to refine course materials, learning formats, and user flows before iterating.

Then I build from theory. For Harvard's Social Technology for Global Aging project I designed an AI conversational model for older adults grounded in Erikson's ego integrity theory and Validation Therapy, structured as a three-stage dialogue: Validation, Meaning, Legacy. The evaluation plan combines psychometric scales with linguistic sentiment analysis so the design can be judged on outcomes, not just impressions.

How I work:

I am comfortable moving between roles. Sometimes I am the researcher coding legislative data or building a risk framework with a Risk Breakdown Structure. Sometimes I am the facilitator running ten-hour online sessions and translating Gestalt psychology into structured activities. Sometimes I am the product person testing an AI companion app and rewriting persona prompts so conversations feel natural to the people using them. And sometimes I build: for the Love BTI relationship-style quiz I researched the questionnaire foundations, designed the questions and result explanations, and used Claude to turn them into an interactive HTML prototype.

What connects these is care for the person on the other side of the screen or the classroom. Whether advising high school students on academic planning or designing for older adults recording their life stories, I want people to feel understood and set up to succeed.

What I'm currently exploring:

I am especially interested in how conversational AI can support emotional well-being and learning without replacing human judgment. At VIBI I refine AI personas and structure coach prompts around four components: who the persona is, how it analyzes user input, how it chooses a response strategy, and what constraints shape the output. I also design feedback loops that bring real user voices back into the product. At Tsinghua I studied the other side of the same question, building a framework for the risks generative AI introduces in higher education. I want to keep working on both: what AI makes possible for learners, and what it takes to do it responsibly.

Beyond work:

I speak fluent Mandarin and English and conversational Korean, and I have studied, lived, and worked in Atlanta, Boston, Beijing, and Nanjing. Moving between cultures is part of why I care about designing experiences that feel local and natural to the people they serve.
