Choose your EBI: module overview

In this module you will find, propose and check one evidence-based intervention (EBI) for a health problem in your own setting. It becomes the foundation of the logic model you build across the course.

Hi, and welcome to Module 2. Last module you wrote your own definitions. This week you make your first real decision: which intervention you want to bring into your setting.

I'll be honest: this is the step where most of my students stumble. The first EBI I ever chose for a project turned out to be a strategy, not an intervention at all. That's normal, and it's why this module gives you a way to check your choice before anyone grades it.

You'll fill in a short template, then ask an AI reviewer to challenge it. Expect it to be tough. Most first choices come back as "workable with changes." That isn't a judgment of you. It's the start of a better project.

Before you start, take two minutes to plan your week with the questions below. See you in the discussion forum.

Teaching + emotional presence: a short, imperfect, personal welcome. The story about the instructor's own mistake makes a hard verdict feel normal.COI checklist ("tell stories," "make mistakes"); Guo et al. 2014: ≤6 min, personal feel beats studio polish

Why this module matters

Interventions that work often take many years to reach the people who need them.

Choosing the right EBI is the first step in closing that gap where you work. A strong choice makes every later piece of your project easier. A weak one is hard to fix later.

Existential presence: a "course why" that connects the module to real people and real stakes.COI checklist 2022 (existential presence)

What you will be able to do

  1. Propose an evidence-based intervention for a health problem in your own setting, using an 8-part template.
  2. Appraise the evidence behind it: its strength, its fit with your setting, and where it sits on the research-to-practice "subway line."
  3. Use an AI reviewer critically: tell apart what came from your text, what is general knowledge, and what you must verify yourself.
  4. Revise and justify your choice in writing, and give useful feedback on a peer's reasoning.
Objectives written as skills a learner can name on a resume. Each one maps to one graded or practice item below.Krauss 2017 (competency-based education); Johns Hopkins benchmark ("goals as skills")

How the project pieces connect

How you'll be assessed

  • Quiz: auto-graded, unlimited attempts.
  • EBI selection: graded by 3 peers using a rubric. They grade your reasoning: what you verified and what you changed.
  • The AI's answer is never graded. Text you submit must be your own.

Where this goes next

  • Your EBI carries into Module 3, where you list barriers in your setting.
  • It becomes the left side of your logic model in Module 6.
  • You'll revisit and revise it in the Module 8 capstone.
Every module adds one piece of a project the learner builds about their own workplace. AI is a sparring partner; the learner's own judgment is what gets graded.Falbel (constructionism); Watkins & Marsick 2020; Deloitte Human Capital Trends 2026
